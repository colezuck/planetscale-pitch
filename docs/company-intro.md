# Opening talk track: company and product context

Verified 2026-09-27. The opening visual stays unchanged; this context belongs in the bullet outline.

- [About PlanetScale](https://planetscale.com/about): founded in 2018 by Vitess co-creators and maintainers. [Vitess introduction](https://planetscale.com/blog/videos-intro-to-vitess-its-powerful-capabilities-and-how-to-get-started): the MySQL scaling project originated at YouTube.
- [Metal announcement](https://planetscale.com/blog/announcing-metal): launched in March 2025, with early production usage measured in trillions of queries and petabytes of storage. [Postgres announcement](https://planetscale.com/blog/planetscale-for-postgres): July 2025 expansion in response to customer demand. This was an expansion alongside Vitess, rather than abandonment of the MySQL platform.
- [Current Metal overview](https://planetscale.com/metal): supports Vitess and Postgres on local NVMe, and names Cash App and Intercom as production customers. The customer credibility refers to the broader Metal platform; it does not imply that these customer workloads use the Postgres engine.
- [Postgres overview](https://planetscale.com/postgres) and [published benchmarks](https://planetscale.com/benchmarks): support a performance claim scoped to the tested configurations. [SLA](https://planetscale.com/legal/planetscale-service-level-agreement): specifies the monthly commitment for eligible HA clusters.

The outline says “high-performance, highly available” and gives benchmark/SLA evidence. “Fastest and most highly available on the market” would combine a workload-dependent performance comparison with an unsupported universal availability ranking. “Mission-critical workloads” conveys the intended operational importance without assigning a formal Tier 0 classification to every customer.
